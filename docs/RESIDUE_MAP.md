# Residue map: where deterministic methods stop in Klareco

This document is the single consolidated map of where deterministic methods stop in Klareco, compiled on 2026-10-17 from evidence already recorded in the repository. `VISION.md` says the accumulated residue characterizations are the thesis deliverable; until now they were spread across the 127 rows of `data/perf/bench_history.jsonl`, the "What the residue looks like so far" section of `VISION.md`, the merge-gate ledger and parser-limits sections of `DESIGN.md`, `docs/PARSER_SEVEN_STEP_RESULTS.md`, `docs/PARSER_RESOURCE_PLAN.md`, `docs/PARSER_EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md`, `docs/PROPER_NOUNS.md`, and GitHub issue comments. The map collects them in one table, one row per capability, grouped as morphology and lexicon, syntax and attachment, retrieval and ranking, and discourse and extraction. It contains no new measurements: every number is copied from a recorded source at the precision that source recorded, with that source's date, and nothing has been rounded, re-derived, or re-run for this document.

Read each row as a claim about the present standing of one capability, not its history. The status column takes exactly one of five values: **HELD** (the deterministic method reached its target), **FALSIFIED-IN-FAVOUR** (a claimed residue shrank or vanished when measured), **CHARACTERIZED** (the residue is measured and described as a property of the problem, and has its own subsection below the table), **UNDER TEST** (the claim has been demoted and waits on data), and **UNMEASURED** (built, but carrying no number). The rule that governs every entry is that it carries a date and an evidence pointer. In a pointer, "ledger" means `data/perf/bench_history.jsonl`, and a ledger row is identified by its timestamp, by its date plus the issue key in its `issue` field, or by its `benchmark` or `experiment` name; a `#NNN` is a GitHub issue in marctjones/klareco; a section title in quotes is a section of the named document. Where two sources disagree, both are recorded with their dates and the newer is marked current. Three caveats apply to every row. The parser rulers, UD-Prago (131 sentences, 2,712 scored tokens) and UD-Cairo (20 sentences, 149 tokens), are development-informed regression fixtures, not held-out gold, and the independently reviewed stratum (#920) has 0 of 200 sentences reviewed; July ledger rows that call Cairo "HELD OUT" predate the 2026-09-08 correction in `DESIGN.md`. The retrieval and extraction numbers were measured against a store built with an older parser, so parser gains do not reach them until the store rebuild (#901, blocked on 21 oversized source rows). And benchmark size changes verdicts: the proper-noun reranker's confidence interval included 0 at n=210 and excluded it at n=500 (2026-07-19), and a real 182,827-sentence parser fix (#871) was invisible on the UD fixtures.

## Characterized residues

Each subsection records the measurement, why the deterministic method cannot close the gap stated as a property of the problem, what information the method provably lacks, and the learned component that `VISION.md` or the ledger nominates for it.

### Residual morpheme OR-nodes after typed-lexicon ranking

**Measurement.** The grammar licenses two or more morpheme readings for 7.48% of running-text tokens; the typed lexicon, selectional restrictions, type hierarchy and Occam collapse 98.67% of those, leaving 0.099% of tokens and 257 word types (2026-07-14T00:30). Wired into the parser with the larger lexicon visible, the residue is 0.285% of tokens (2026-07-14T05:30).

**Why the deterministic method cannot close it.** The surviving pattern is the pejorative prefix `fi-` colliding with roots that begin with `fi-`: `filino` is fil+in (daughter) or fi-lin (contemptible flax). Both readings are grammatically legal and both satisfy every selectional restriction, because `fi-` demands a noun and `lin` is a noun, while `fil` is animate and `-in` demands an animate. In the ledger's words, "the grammar did its job perfectly and returned two answers" (2026-07-14T00:30).

**What it provably lacks.** Knowledge of which of two well-formed lexemes exists in usage: "only knowing that daughters exist and contemptible flax does not separates them." The forest already reserves `fonto = modelo` for a collapse made by a model, so the slot for the missing information is in the data contract.

**Nominated learned component.** The ledger: this "is exactly where a learned ranker belongs" (2026-07-14T00:30). The external analogue is Guinard 2016's Markov model over morpheme semantic classes (98.9%). No such ranker has been built, and at 0.099% of tokens this is the smallest residue on the map.

### Prepositional-phrase attachment (nmod vs obl)

**Measurement.** 54 of 720 PP attachments (7.5%) are emitted as OR-nodes because the gold itself splits about 2:1 for en, al, per and kiel (2026-07-14T03:30). The `al` split is 21 nmod against 18 obl, "a coin flip", and the project stated the irreducible part of nmod at about 15% (2026-07-14). nmod reached 60% against a measured deterministic ceiling of 85% in July and is still the largest Prago error class at 145 tokens (2026-09-05). 99.1% of recurring verb+preposition pairs have the same resolved-or-ambiguous status in every document they appear in (2026-09-08).

**Why the deterministic method cannot close it.** The grammar licenses both attachments and is correct to: no case ending, no agreement, and no rule in the 16 separates `vidis la viron kun teleskopo` attached to the seeing from the same phrase attached to the man (#826). The 2026-09-08 result adds that the ambiguity does not vary with document context either, so the deciding information is neither in the sentence's morphology nor in the surrounding discourse.

**What it provably lacks.** Lexical association between the governing verb or noun and the preposition, which is the information Hindle and Rooth 1993 used to reach about 80% from co-occurrence counts alone (#826; review section 8.5). The September candidate audit adds a second, more mundane gap: for most remaining nmod errors the gold head is not even among the recorded candidates, 5 of 58 PP records on Prago (#914, 2026-09-05).

**Nominated learned component.** #826: a learned ranker acting on exactly the 7.5% of attachments that are OR-nodes and on nothing else. Before that, docs/PARSER_RESOURCE_PLAN.md and the review (sections 8.5 and 10.4) nominate a deterministic-adjacent step: a corpus association table over verb-preposition-noun triples counted from document-disjoint text, plus a versioned valency resource (#924, #925), with any model confined to ranking existing candidates.

### Lexical synonymy via the ontology (verb classes, class tokens, ReVo SINONIMO)

**Measurement.** The hand-seeded verb-class signal moved MRR by 0.0000 (2026-07-14; delta 0.0, p=1.0 on qa_gold_v2, 2026-07-17). Ontology class tokens in the index were within noise (2026-07-17). ReVo SINONIMO expansion fired on 137 of 147 synonym-residue questions (93%) with a mean of 5.1 synonyms each and moved recall by +1 at 200 and 0 at 5 and 20 (2026-07-18, #873). The bucket is 147 of 506 deep-band misses (29%), the single largest cause (2026-07-17, #854).

**Why the deterministic method cannot close it.** Coverage is not the limiter, relevance is. The relation between the question's wording and the answer's is not synonymy: the taxonomy's own headline example, `posedis` and `vendas` (own and sell), is a converse relation, and the rest is world-knowledge paraphrase; a synonym table cannot bridge either (#873, #874). `DESIGN.md` adds the scale problem on the verb-class side: hand-enumerating 39,718 verb roots into semantic classes "is not a plausible deterministic project".

**What it provably lacks.** A non-lexical match between question and answer: knowledge that two predicates describe one event from different participants' sides, or that one stated fact entails the asked one. #874, still open, is the audit that would say how much of the 147 is lexical even in principle.

**Nominated learned component.** #868, re-scoped on 2026-07-18 from "learned lexical synonymy" to "learned inferential paraphrase / converse-relation matching": dense retrieval or a learned paraphrase matcher, evaluated against these 147 questions with the trivial and rerankable bands as controls. The 2026-07-19 scorecard names dense retrieval in the Phase-3 shadow harness (#893, #894) as "the point where, by the method, machine learning finally belongs". `VISION.md` still lists this residue as claimed rather than earned; the ledger's position since #873 is that the deterministic lever is exhausted.

### Deep-band recall where question and answer share few content words

**Measurement.** 136 of 506 deep misses are low_overlap; 98 of them (72%) have their anchor in the gold sentence's own article, and 38 are truly irreducible within a single sentence (2026-07-17, #867). Article-title union recovers 15 of 136 into the pool and 0 of 136 into the extractor's top-20, and the frozen benchmark is flat (2026-07-18, #870). Deep-band recall@200 is 36% on rebaseline_210 (2026-07-18), deep-band MRR is about 0.07 for every arm (2026-07-17), and deep-band answer accuracy is 8.4% on rebaseline_500 (2026-07-19).

**Why the deterministic method cannot close it.** "Sharing too few content words with the question is the band's DEFINITION" (#870). Recovery into the pool is possible, but the recovered gold cannot be ranked by any lexical method: union candidates enter at one flat synthetic rank, and promoting them promotes about 89 wrong article-introduction siblings per anchor. Enlarging the window does not help either, 8 of 1,038 (2026-07-18, #857).

**What it provably lacks.** A question-to-answer similarity that does not depend on shared terms. For the 38 irreducible cases nothing in the gold sentence or its article names the question's anchor.

**Nominated learned component.** #870: "surfacing it needs non-lexical question<->answer matching. This localizes precisely where a learned component belongs." Review section 10.4 and the 2026-07-19 scorecard: dense retrieval for the deep band in shadow mode, evaluated against the 147 inferential-paraphrase questions and the 38 irreducible ones with the deterministic floor still running so the delta is attributable. A multilingual encoder off the shelf is named as the cheap first arm and a small bi-encoder trained on the corpus as the thesis-consistent second.

### Reranking with AST structure

**Measurement.** On the honest 1,345-question set, the arc-level tree reranker beats BM25 by +0.0057 and the clause-frame reranker by +0.0120, both with confidence intervals that exclude 0, while the clause-frame reranker is significantly worse than BM25 (-0.0063) and the whole-AST reranker, once it actually ran, lost 0.0173 MRR and was demoted (2026-07-17, 2026-07-18). A perfect subject/object role match lifted 0 of 36 in-pool golds into the top 5 (2026-07-18, #866). Every reranker is flat at about 0.07 MRR on the deep band (2026-07-17).

**Why the deterministic method cannot close it.** Two properties are recorded. First, on the questions where BM25 fails and reranking could matter, the question is a subject gap with a single object constraint, exactly the (subject, verb, object) frame, and its subject is the interrogative, so a role match has no content radiko to align on (2026-07-14, 2026-07-18). Second, the gold shares the frame and the common terms with thousands of candidates; what separates it is the rare entity, which is lexical and which #877 exploits for 5 of 35 in-pool golds, or nothing in the sentence at all, which is the deep band.

**What it provably lacks.** Structure carries no information about which of several structurally identical sentences answers the question; the discriminating information is lexical specificity or world knowledge. The arc-level tree's unique power, a modifier-arc constraint such as `la libron DE Petro`, was never exercised because no benchmark question carries one (2026-07-14, recorded as F13).

**Nominated learned component.** None for ranking itself. `VISION.md` leaves open whether the tie-breaker is "information-theoretic specificity, which we have not yet exploited -- versus learned calibration". The ledger routes the remaining headroom to first-stage recall (the two residues above) and to the unexploited deterministic levers in the two UNMEASURED retrieval rows. Review section 8.4 notes the result matches the TREC-era conclusion that linguistic structure buys little in retrieval.

### Answer-span extraction (#869)

**Measurement.** Against the passage-returning baseline (token_f1 0.0148, contains_gold 0.312), the aggressive cascade gains +0.0336 token_f1 and loses 0.200 contains_gold; the conservative role-span extractor gains +0.0189 and loses 0.069 while firing on 26% of questions; the strict proper-noun gate gains +0.0055 and loses 0.019 while firing on 7% (2026-07-18, 2026-07-19). On rebaseline_500 the pipeline scores answer accuracy 0.266, exact_match 0.0, token_f1 0.0119 (2026-07-19).

**Why the deterministic method cannot close it.** "recall@1 is ~28%, so whenever the top-1 passage is not the gold, extracting its span can only hurt or be neutral; no confidence gate can know the gold is present without it being retrieved to rank-1" (2026-07-19, `#869 DEFINITIVE`). The contains regression therefore shrinks only as the fire rate shrinks and approaches zero without reaching it.

**What it provably lacks.** Whether the passage it is extracting from contains the answer at all, which is a retrieval property, not an extraction one. A secondary gap: the gold is not always in the grammatical role the interrogative predicts (subordinate-clause answers, parser noise).

**Nominated learned component.** None for extraction. The ledger makes #869 downstream of retrieval@1: deep-band recall (#25, which is the dense-retrieval residue above) and Reranker v2 (#26, whose shipped proper-noun boost raised recall@5 but not answer accuracy). A deterministic per-question fitness gate is expected to ship contains-neutrally only for a subset, WHO questions with a proper-noun filler.

## What this map does not yet say

Nine rows carry no verdict. Each is listed with the single measurement it waits on.

- **Proper-noun inference without a gazetteer** (UNDER TEST). #820: a proper-noun evaluation set of at least 2,000 gold PROPN tokens in context, typed by Wikidata, stratified so the capitalized, valid-morphology, known-root class (`Petro`, `Nordo`, `Brita`) is present; then #819's deterministic ladder re-run against it. Forty-one tokens cannot size the residue.
- **Word-sense disambiguation** (UNMEASURED). A sense-labelled in-context sample that turns the 32.46% polysemous-root upper bound into a disambiguation burden (Bick's 3.8% is the reference), which itself needs a single-sentence WSD mechanism first (2026-09-08).
